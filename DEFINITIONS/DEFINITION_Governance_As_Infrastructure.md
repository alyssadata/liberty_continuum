# DEFINITION — Governance as Infrastructure

**Status:** Working-branch expansion of the earlier placeholder. Historical tagged releases remain intact.

## Definition
Governance as infrastructure is the arrangement of public rights, accountable authority, modular service provision, and independent assurance into inspectable systems whose rules, decisions, and consequences can be challenged and corrected.

## Required properties
- **Rights supremacy:** Service optimization cannot silently displace protected rights.
- **Bounded authority:** Every consequential decision class has a public, jurisdiction-aware authorization path and accountable owner.
- **Separation of responsibility:** Service execution, appeal review, audit, and authority to restore frozen systems should not collapse into the same unreviewable actor.
- **Visibility with privacy:** Public rules, versions, and accountability; protected personal data exposed only on an authorized need-to-know basis.
- **Modularity and replaceability:** Services can be changed or removed without erasing records, rights, or recourse.
- **Correctability:** Impacted people can receive reasons, challenge evidence, seek review, and obtain effective remedy.
- **Tripwires:** Loss of required provenance, audit, or appeal capacity reduces automation scope or freezes execution until independent restoration.

## Interface to the proposed vNext layers
- L0 — Rights and constitutional invariants
- L1 — Governance and authority
- L2 — Service and execution
- L3 — Independent assurance: provenance, audit, appeal, measurement, anti-capture
- L4 — Transition, recovery, and revision

## Evaluation
For a specified consequential decision, identify who may authorize it, what recorded rule applies, where the evidence lives, how an affected party contests the result, what freezes authority, and who independently verifies recovery. A failure to supply one of these interfaces is a documented gap, not a silently assumed permission.

## Boundary
This framework proposes engineering and governance requirements. It does not itself confer legal powers, replace statutory or constitutional law, or certify any deployment.
